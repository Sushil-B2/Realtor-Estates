A considered purchase starts with good questions

The grounded plot buyer’s checklist

A practical field guide for checking the paperwork, the land and the promises—before a plot becomes a payment.

Use this as a prompt list, not legal advice. Rules and records vary by parcel and jurisdiction. Ask an independent Maharashtra property lawyer to review original documents and confirm the latest records with the relevant authority.

Before you visit

  1. Ask for the exact plot reference.Get the plot number, phase, sanctioned layout sheet, measured area and boundary dimensions in writing. Check that every document points to the same parcel.
  2. Verify the title chain and seller authority.Ask a lawyer to review the ownership history, current owner, authority to sell and any powers of attorney or agreements.
  3. Check land use and layout permissions.Confirm the relevant zoning/land-use status, final sanctioned layout and any conditions or reservations directly with the appropriate planning authority.
  4. Search for encumbrances and disputes.Review current encumbrance records, registered interests, acquisition notices, court disputes, mortgages and other restrictions with professional help.
  5. Review revenue and mutation records.Ask your adviser which current 7/12 extract, property card, mutation entries or other local records apply to this parcel; reconcile them with the title papers.
  6. Check RERA applicability.Ask whether registration is required for this project and, if so, independently verify the live registration details and disclosures on the official MahaRERA portal.

When you are on site

  1. Walk the actual boundaries.Use a qualified surveyor if needed. Compare visible corner markers, access and plot dimensions with the approved plan—never rely only on a brochure graphic.
  2. Inspect access and services today.Check the approach road, internal road condition, drainage, water, electricity and boundary work in person. Ask which are complete, proposed or pending, and request dated evidence.
  3. Test the map against the ground.Confirm the precise location, route and surrounding uses independently. Treat proposed roads, stations or public projects as unconfirmed until the responsible agency verifies them.
  4. Ask about plot-specific restrictions.Check setbacks, easements, reservations, utility corridors, nala/buffer lines, road-widening deductions and building permissions for the exact lot.

Before any payment

  1. Get the complete written cost.Request the base price, taxes, registration/stamp duty, development or maintenance charges, payment milestones, refund terms and any other fees—no verbal-only promises.
  2. Read the agreement before signing.Have an independent lawyer check the exact land description, area, possession terms, seller obligations, remedies and refund conditions.
  3. Verify the recipient and receipt.Pay only the verified legal seller through traceable channels against an authorised receipt. Never transfer money to an unverified personal account.
  4. Keep copies and follow up.Save signed documents, receipts, sanctioned plans, correspondence and official search results. Ask your lawyer about registration and post-purchase mutation steps.
